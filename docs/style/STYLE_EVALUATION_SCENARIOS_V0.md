# STYLE_EVALUATION_SCENARIOS_V0

**Status:** draft — first evaluation set from Issue #2 style discovery.

These scenarios are not outfit prescriptions. They are test cases for determining whether Moirai can distinguish a generically acceptable outfit from one Sil would actually choose, enjoy and repeat.

## Evaluation rule

For each proposed outfit, evaluate at least:

1. **Would Sil actually wear it?**
2. **Is it appropriate for the stated context?**
3. **Does it feel like Sil rather than generic styling advice?**
4. **Is it practical enough for the situation?**
5. **Does it accidentally read as too serious, too classic, too plain or too dressed-up?**
6. **Is unfamiliarity caused by true dislike or only by low combination confidence?**
7. **Are omitted accessories genuinely unnecessary, or were they simply forgotten?**
8. **Is the chosen degree of formality appropriate, or is the system merely reproducing Sil's current low-effort default?**

A novel combination must not be rejected merely because Sil would not have invented it herself. Likewise, an outfit that resembles current habits must not automatically be treated as optimal for the context.

---

## S01 — Ordinary professional day

**Context:** normal workday, no special ceremony or highly formal meeting.

**Target:** professional, current, relaxed, visibly intentional.

**Likely-good ingredients:**
- slim/skinny, chino, tailored no-pleat or ankle-length trousers;
- colourful blouse, knit, cardigan, short jacket or relaxed blazer;
- ankle boot, polished sandal or modern smart sneaker depending weather/context;
- colour should normally be visible somewhere;
- habitual silver jewellery can remain by default;
- optional belt when it materially improves the outfit.

**Likely-failure modes:**
- dark neutral outfit with no personality;
- rigid corporate suit language;
- sneaker chosen automatically merely to make the outfit “relaxed”;
- wide-leg, straight-leg or high-waisted trousers proposed as trend correction.

**Open calibration question:** the work/casual overlap is descriptive evidence, not yet proof that the current ordinary-work baseline is optimally formal. During the wardrobe pilot, compare Sil's habitual work outfits with slightly more polished alternatives and learn from real preference and context appropriateness.

## S02 — Casual-professional crossover

**Context:** workday where jeans/chinos and a more casual upper layer are acceptable.

**Target:** outfit could plausibly work for both work and an after-work casual plan without a full change.

**Known evidence:** Sil explicitly accepted several casual-reference looks for work as well.

**Likely-good ingredients:**
- jeans or chinos;
- colourful knit, blouse, cardigan, distinctive jacket or relaxed blazer;
- ankle boot, sandal or modern sneaker;
- one visible styling move can provide polish: structured layer, better shoe, belt, jewellery or deliberate colour combination.

**Test:** Moirai should raise or lower polish without changing Sil's core identity.

---

## S03 — Professional without blazer

**Context:** workday where wearing a blazer would feel repetitive or unnecessary.

**Target:** clearly work-appropriate without falling back to formal tailoring.

**Validated routes:**
- colourful/printed blouse + trousers;
- short structured non-blazer jacket + trousers;
- cardigan/knit + shirt/top + slim trousers.

**Failure condition:** claiming that professionalism requires a blazer.

---

## S04 — Guided colour expansion

**Context:** Sil wants something less predictable than what she would combine herself.

**Target:** use colour confidently without turning novelty into costume.

**Rules:**
- strong colour + clean base is validated;
- one colourful statement piece + neutral base is validated;
- colour-on-colour is allowed as an exploratory suggestion;
- darker palettes can work, but avoid unnecessarily dim or heavy overall energy.

**Critical distinction:**
“I would not have thought of this” is not equivalent to “I dislike this.”

---

## S05 — Accessory-memory assist

**Context:** base outfit already works but could benefit from a finishing element.

**Target:** surface one useful accessory without turning dressing into a checklist.

**Rules:**
- silver jewellery is default-compatible;
- rose gold is acceptable;
- yellow gold is excluded;
- belts may be suggested because forgetting them is a known friction point;
- bag/carrying suggestions should remain optional until a suitable low-friction format is validated.

**Failure condition:** inferring that low historical use means dislike.

---

## S06 — Casual / weekend

**Context:** ordinary leisure, errands, meeting someone casually, day out.

**Target:** same core identity as work, with less required polish.

**Known positive language:**
- jeans/chinos;
- colourful cardigan or knit;
- colourful/distinctive jacket;
- blouse or tee;
- ankle boots, sandals or modern sneakers;
- visible colour and personality.

**Important:** casual should not be modelled as a separate aesthetic identity. It is usually a lower-formality version of the same style language.

---

## S07 — Work-to-dinner transition

**Status:** V0 direction validated; real-wardrobe calibration still required.

**Context:** work followed by dinner or a somewhat more polished evening plan.

**Known constraints and evidence:**
- dresses and skirts remain excluded;
- heeled ankle boots and heeled shoes are viable;
- Sil does not want to feel excessively dressed-up;
- all three tested dinner/event references were acceptable, including stronger colour, statement blazer/jacket and heels.

**V0 interpretation:** Sil tolerates a meaningful increase in evening polish without needing to switch to a different aesthetic identity. Colour and statement layers remain compatible at the higher-formality end.

**Pilot test:** determine from real garments whether the preferred transition is usually achieved by changing shoes/layer/accessories or by changing more of the outfit.

---

## S08 — Conference / speaking / visible professional event

**Status:** V0 direction validated; real-wardrobe calibration still required.

**Context:** presenting, facilitating, conference, professional event with higher visibility.

**Known evidence:**
- professional, colourful and current remains the target;
- trousers preferred;
- all three tested higher-visibility references were acceptable: full colour tailoring, colour + print, and the more relaxed jeans/blazer end;
- therefore neither a full coloured suit nor a relaxed polished formula is intrinsically outside Sil's style.

**V0 interpretation:** the acceptable range is broader than first assumed. The key decision is not “formal or informal?” but **what degree of polish fits this specific event while still feeling like Sil**.

**Pilot test:** use event context (audience, venue, role, visibility, travel, dinner afterwards) to choose the appropriate point on the formality dial.

---

## S09 — Formality calibration / current-habit challenge

**Status:** required during real-wardrobe pilot.

**Context:** any work scenario where a habitual outfit and a slightly more polished alternative are both plausible.

**Purpose:** distinguish a genuine style preference for cross-context dressing from a current habit driven by convenience, low combination confidence or low decision effort.

**Test method:**
1. propose one outfit close to Sil's current habitual baseline;
2. propose one alternative using the same core aesthetic but one step higher in polish;
3. keep both practical and recognisably Sil;
4. collect preference and, when actually worn, post-wear feedback.

**Do not pre-judge the answer.** The objective is to learn whether Sil wants more polish on ordinary workdays, not to “correct” her toward conventional office formality.

---

## Negative controls

The following should normally fail unless Sil explicitly requests an experiment:

1. dress or skirt as ordinary recommendation;
2. wide-leg trouser as default trend-led advice;
3. high-waisted trouser as default recommendation;
4. straight-leg trouser as default recommendation;
5. ballet flat;
6. loafer as assumed wardrobe-gap solution;
7. yellow-gold jewellery;
8. all-neutral, sober corporate outfit when colour/personality could reasonably be introduced;
9. repeated use of sneakers as the universal method for making an outfit relaxed;
10. an outfit that is technically fashionable but makes Sil feel too serious, too classic, too plain or too dressed-up.

## V0 pass criterion

Moirai passes this first style-authority layer when it can consistently:

- propose real-looking combinations within these boundaries;
- distinguish context through degree of polish rather than identity switching;
- introduce combinations Sil would not invent herself without drifting outside her preferences;
- respect hard exclusions;
- surface forgotten-but-welcome finishing elements selectively;
- distinguish preference from habit/convenience when calibrating formality;
- explain why an outfit works in one or two useful sentences rather than fashion jargon.
